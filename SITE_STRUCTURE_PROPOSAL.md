# The Website, Kept Small

**Website recommendation, 26 August 2026**

My recommendation for the Allen Legal Nurse Consultants website: keep it small, make
it easy to find, and sharpen what is already there rather than building something
larger. Below is where the site stands today, what I would change, the full page list
with what goes on each page and how it helps, and the few items I need confirmed
before I start.

---

## 1. Where the site stands today

The site has **18 pages** today: home, solutions, services, technology and delivery,
about, process, resources, contact; three solution detail pages (chronology, case
screening, DME observation); three resource pages (LNC guide, timeline worksheet,
attorney intake); and four legal pages (privacy, terms, accessibility,
confidentiality). It is clean, professional, and covers the basics. Two things stand
out to me:

1. **It reads as a general legal nurse consultant brochure.** It does not say plainly
   what you are known for: the niche work (board of nursing defense, NP and PA
   malpractice, IME and DME observation), the states you cover, or the faster
   AI-assisted turnaround. Someone searching for one of those needs, rather than for
   you by name, finds nothing on the site that matches.

2. **The other direction is to make the site much larger.** One option is to build 15
   or more new landing pages, one for every specialty, every state, and every buyer
   type, as a full search-optimization project. I looked at that and I do not think
   we need it yet. It is more site than the goal calls for, and more to write and
   keep current.

## 2. What I want the site to do

Here is the job as I see it:

- Be the clear, professional page people land on when they search you or the firm by
  name, or follow a referral, and make it obvious how to reach you by phone or email.
- Be clear and factual enough that an AI assistant (ChatGPT, Google's AI answers, and
  similar) can state what you do, who you help, and where.
- Stay small and easy to keep current.
- Not be a lead-generation funnel with a separate page for every state and city, at
  least not now.

If that is the job, the site does not need to get bigger. It needs to get sharper.

## 3. What I recommend

Your current shape already matches what I would recommend, so this is tightening, not
a rebuild. The changes:

- **Remove Solutions** and move its content into **Services**. The two pages carry the
  same message.
- **Remove Technology and Delivery** as its own page. Move the parts worth keeping
  (secure workflow, source-linked references, defined scope) into Services as a short
  "How the work is delivered" section.
- **Keep Process.** It sets expectations and shows the confidentiality steps, which
  helps an attorney decide to engage.
- **Check Attorney Intake against Contact.** Both collect the same kind of
  information. Keep one.
- **Keep the three detail pages.** They already follow the right pattern, and **DME
  and IME Observation** is one of the niche pages either way. It gets sharpened, not
  rebuilt.
- **Add BON Defense Consulting** and **NP and PA Malpractice Review** as their own
  pages, in the same pattern you already use.
- **Add one Service Areas** page covering Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina on a
  single page, not one page per state.

I recommend the three new pages because the market research points straight at them:

- **Board of nursing defense** is described as an underdeveloped, high-margin market
  with steady, non-litigation demand, and almost no consultant has a page for it.
- **NP and PA malpractice** is the fastest-growing malpractice vertical, with high
  claim values, and the dual standard-of-care analysis it needs is something most
  reviewers cannot do.
- **Service areas** is where the states live as real text, which is what search and AI
  assistants match on. Your research calls for state-level pages; one consolidated page
  delivers most of that value at a fraction of the upkeep, and it answers "do you cover
  my state" directly.

Most of this already exists. Your five core pages and three specialty pages are
built. The net new work is three pages: BON Defense, NP and PA Malpractice, and
Service Areas. Two pages come out (Solutions folds into Services, Technology and
Delivery folds into Services), so the total barely moves. The leaner option is to
make BON and NP and PA sections on the Services page rather than their own pages,
which brings the net new work down to one, the Service Areas page. I lean toward the
full three, because being found for that work is the point.

**How this compares.** I looked at other legal nurse consultant sites. Solo
consultants run four to eight navigable pages (Home, About, Services, Contact, and
sometimes Credentials, Testimonials, Clients). Firms that market themselves run ten to
eighteen, and the ones that build a page per service, such as OnPoint LNC, sit at the
top of that range. Sites with far more are marketing-heavy law firms building a page
per practice area and per city, which is the build this avoids. Your own research
positions you as an agency rather than a solo consultant, so a firm-tier site of
roughly a dozen pages is on strategy, and since most of it is already built the actual
work is small.

Reference sites: dlcasonconsulting.com (solo, 4 pages), brantlnc.com (solo, 7 to 8),
alexanlnc.com (firm, about 6 plus staff bios), legalnurseusa.com (firm, 10 to 12),
onpointlnc.com (firm, 15 to 18, a page per service).

It lists all three degrees and schools (Western Governors University, Post University,

What it is missing is the part that turns a resume into a reason to call: a few plain

## 6. On search and the states

A page ranks for "legal nurse consultant Georgia" because that page is about legal
nurse consulting in Georgia. Search work added later cannot create a place the site
never mentions. For location searches, the content is the search optimization.

We do not need to rank for every location to meet the goal. Three things do the real
work:

- **Name searches**, where someone who already heard of you looks you up. A clean
  domain and a strong About page cover it, and the site nearly does already.
- **Near-me searches**, which are won mostly through a Google Business Profile (the
  business listing with a map, service area, and reviews), not through pages. It is a
  free listing rather than a content project, and it takes a few weeks to verify, so
  it is worth starting now rather than last.
- **Naming Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina in plain sentences** on Home and
  About. A few sentences, in a few places, give Google and AI assistants something
  concrete, with no page sprawl.

So the polish side of search work can wait. Two things I would not wait on: claim the
Google Business Profile, and decide now which services and states get named on the
site.

### Where the states appear on the site

Your instinct is right: the states have to show up as real text so search engines and
AI assistants have something to match and to quote. Here is where:

- **Home**, one line near the top: "serving attorneys in Georgia, Florida, and North
  Carolina."
- **About**, in the plain-language summary at the top of the page.
- **The Service Areas page**, a paragraph per state with the metros and courts you
  cover.
- **The footer**, site-wide: "Serving Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina."
- **Structured data**, the machine-readable block in the page code, in the
  `areaServed` field. A one-time addition.
- **Google Business Profile**, in the service-area setting.

Visible sentences matter most, because that is what an AI assistant reads. Structured
data and page titles help search engines and can be tuned later. If we later decide to
go after attorney traffic in Florida and North Carolina specifically, that is when
more location pages start to earn their place, and they can be added then with no
rework.

## 8. Before I build

None of this needs to be decided today. These are the open items:

**Goal and scope**

1. Is the site's job only to be found and contacted, or do you also want it bringing
   in new attorney leads over the next year? This decides how much we add.
2. How lean do you want it? My recommended version is roughly a dozen navigable pages,
   which matches a marketing-oriented firm. The leaner version makes BON and NP and PA
   sections on the Services page instead of their own pages, landing closer to nine or
   ten, the size of a typical solo consultant site. Either way it is far smaller than a
   page-per-state build.

**Positioning**

3. Is the name "Allen Legal Nurse Consultants" everywhere? One of the strategy decks
   refers to "Purview," so I want to confirm that is dropped.
4. Which states go on the site: Georgia, Florida, and North Carolina, or Georgia,
   North Carolina, South Carolina, Alabama, and Tennessee? The two decks differ.
5. What leads the message: AI-assisted and faster, or senior clinical judgment? A
   blend is fine, I just need to know which one is the headline.

**Services**

6. Do you want to actively market board of nursing defense and NP and PA malpractice?
   If yes, each gets its own page. If not, they stay as items on the Services page.

**Findability**

7. Are you comfortable with a Google Business Profile, a public listing with the
   business name, service area, phone, and client reviews? Any concern about showing
   a service area rather than a street address?
8. A simple contact form, or strictly phone and email?

**Content I need from you**

9. Any anonymized case examples or client quotes I can use? The research decks make
   the point that attorneys buy trust before they buy services.
10. Are the downloadable PDFs (welcome packet, fee schedule, intake bundle) final, or
    placeholders?

**Timing**

11. Is this a tighten-what-exists project of a week or two, or a build-it-out project
    over a couple of months?

---

My recommendation is to keep your structure, sharpen the five pages that carry the
message, add the three the research supports, and start the Google Business Profile
now. Everything else can follow once we agree on the picture.
